<Section number="01" title="Decrement baskets — payoff geometry">

Many **autocallable notes** on a **basket** combine a **worst-of** (or rank-dependent) underlying with a **decrement** mechanism: the conditional coupon is reduced (or memory is lost) when any name in the basket crosses a barrier schedule. The effective state variable is not the basket average but a **path-dependent functional** of the minima (or order statistics) of the components.

Let $M_t = \min_i \bigl(S_i(t)/S_i(0)\bigr)$ denote the **normalized worst-of** level. A stylized **decrement** coupon at observation $T_k$ can be written as:

<MathBlock label="Decrement coupon (stylized)" number="1.1">

$$
\text{Coupon}_k = C_{\max} - \Delta C \cdot \sum_{j \leq k} \mathbf{1}\left[\min_{t \in [T_{j-1},T_j]} M_t \leq B_j\right]
$$

</MathBlock>

with floors/caps omitted for clarity. The key observation: **any skew in the joint distribution of $(S_1,\ldots,S_n)$ near barriers feeds directly into decrement probabilities** — not only ATM basket vol.

<Callout type="insight">
The **basket implied skew** relevant for decrement products is dominated by the **lower tail** of the joint distribution (worst-of events), not by the weighted-average stock skew used for dispersion-style basket options.
</Callout>

### Mapping to market data

Vanillas on individual names give **marginal smiles**; index and multi-name exotics pin **implied correlation** and sometimes **correlation skew**. The task is to build a **consistent basket smile** for $M_t$ (or for a basket level $B_t = \sum_i w_i S_i$ when the payoff mixes both).

</Section>

<Section number="02" title="Implied skew of the worst-of">

Consider $n$ names with log-returns $X_i$ at horizon $T$. The **worst-of log-return** is $X_{\min} = \min_i X_i$. For Gaussian copula / jointly normal shocks with correlation matrix $\rho$:

<MathBlock label="Worst-of variance (Gaussian copula, equal vol)" number="2.1">

$$
\sigma_{\min}^2 \approx \sigma^2 \left(1 - \frac{2}{\pi}(1-\bar\rho)\right)
$$

</MathBlock>

where $\bar\rho$ is a representative average pairwise correlation (exact formulas use quadrature over order statistics). More importantly, the **left tail** of $X_{\min}$ is **fatter** than that of any single name when $\rho<1$, but **thinner** than independent names — correlation controls tail pooling.

**Implied skew** for options on $M_T$ is driven by:

1. **Marginal skew** of each $S_i$ (via $X_i$ asymmetry).
2. **Dependence structure** (copula / correlation skew) governing co-jumps into the worst-of.
3. **Barrier sampling** — discrete monitoring **lifts** hitting probabilities vs continuous models.

<Callout type="warning">
A common modelling trap is to price decrement baskets using **ATM basket vol** plus a **single-name skew overlay**. Near barriers, the **conditional law** of $M_t$ given *no prior decrement* is materially different from the unconditional law.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="03" title="From single-name smiles to basket skew">

A practical recipe used on desks:

### Step 1 — Marginals

Calibrate each name to a parametric smile (SVI, SABR slice, or stochastic vol) so that $\sigma_i(K,T)$ is smooth and arbitrage-free.

### Step 2 — Copula / correlation

Choose a correlation matrix $\rho_{ij}(T)$ (possibly state-dependent). For **worst-of skew**, stress **basis correlation** scenarios: lowering $\rho_{ij}$ **increases** the mass on *divergent* paths where one name lags — often **increasing decrement risk** when barriers are name-specific.

### Step 3 — Basket / min smile extraction

Simulate $(S_1,\ldots,S_n)$ under the joint model; build the smile of $M_T$ (or of a basket index) by regression on vanilla payoffs or by FFT if a tractable characteristic function exists.

<MathBlock label="Heuristic skew transfer" number="3.1">

$$
\mathcal{S}_{M}(k) \;\approx\; \min_i \mathcal{S}_{i}(k) \;+\; \Phi(\rho,\kappa_3^{(i)},\text{barrier geometry})
$$

</MathBlock>

where $\mathcal{S}$ denotes a skew measure (e.g. 25Δ RR) and $\Phi$ collects **co-skew** adjustments from Gaussian or Edgeworth expansions.

<Callout type="note">
For **equal barriers** on all names, the worst-of event is symmetric in labels; for **heterogeneous barriers**, the skew is **concentrated** on the tightest names — always sort names by **barrier distance in local vol units** before reduction formulas.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="04" title="Forward skew and decrement conditionals">

Decrement structures are **path-dependent**: conditioning on *no decrement so far* removes mass from trajectories that dipped near barriers. The **forward smile** relevant for the next period is therefore **conditioned**:

$$
\sigma_{\text{impl}}^{\text{fwd}}(k) \;\text{ under }\; \mathbb{P}\bigl(\cdot \mid \forall j<k,\ \min_{[T_{j-1},T_j]} M_t > B_j\bigr)
$$

**Local volatility** models tend to **flatten** this forward skew rapidly; **LSV** and **stochastic vol** preserve more forward smile and typically shift **decrement probabilities** vs LV (see the companion note on autocall hedging).

<Section number="05" title="Implementation checklist">

| Item | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Marginal smiles + no-arb checks | Stable $\sigma_i(K,T)$ for simulation |
| Correlation / copula stress grid | Bounds on worst-of tail |
| Barrier monitoring (discrete) | Coupon step and PRDC-style discontinuities |
| Forward skew under survival | Conditionals for remaining coupons |
| Model reserve (LV vs LSV) | Capture forward smile uncertainty |

<Callout type="result" title="Desk summary">
For **decrement baskets**, hedge ratios to **single-name skew** are **concentrated** in the names closest to barriers in **local-vol distance**. A robust book aggregates **skew sensitivities by barrier cohort**, not by notional weight alone.
</Callout>

</Section>
