<Section number="01" title="Why autocall hedging is model-dependent">

An **autocall** redeems early when the underlying (or a basket functional) is above barriers on observation dates; coupons often depend on prior **survival**. Hedging uses vanillas and spot — but the **true** risks are **path-dependent** combinations of:

- **Barrier transition probabilities** between observation dates
- **Forward skew** and **forward vol** of the underlying conditional on survival
- **Convexity in vol** (volga) and **spot–vol correlation** (vanna) when exotics are priced with smiles

<MathBlock label="Survival event" number="1.1">

$$
\mathcal{A}_k = \left\{ S_{T_j} < B_j,\ \forall j < k \right\},\qquad
p_k = \mathbb{P}\left( S_{T_k} \geq B_k \,\middle|\, \mathcal{A}_k \right)
$$

</MathBlock>

**Local volatility (LV)** and **local stochastic volatility (LSV)** can match **today’s vanilla surface** yet imply **different dynamics** for how that surface evolves — hence **different hedge ratios** for the same autocall NPV.

<Callout type="insight">
Hedging an autocall is not only **delta–gamma** management. A large part of unexplained P&L comes from **smile dynamics** that LV and LSV predict differently — especially **between observation dates**.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="02" title="Hedging under local volatility">

In Dupire LV, spot follows $dS/S = \cdots + \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(S,t)\,dW$. The model has **no independent stochastic volatility factor**; future smiles are **functionals** of today’s entire surface.

### Consequences for hedging

- **Vega** is primarily **spot- and time-local**: re-hedging vanillas can **overfit** today's surface while **missing forward buckets** that matter for barrier survival.
- **Volga** from a pure diffusion LV perspective is often **muted** compared to what traders observe when the market smile **moves in a correlated way** with spot.
- **Vanna** is **encoded** in how $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ changes with $S$; it is not a free parameter matched to historical smile dynamics.

<Callout type="warning">
A desk hedging an autocall purely to **LV bucketed vega** may run a **structural short vol-of-vol** vs realized smile moves — the hedge **looks** flat day one but bleeds through observation periods.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="03" title="Hedging under LSV">

LSV combines a stochastic variance process $V_t$ with a **leverage function** $L(S,t)$ so that **all European vanillas** reprice:

<MathBlock label="LSV spot dynamics" number="3.1">

$$
\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = \mu_t\,dt + L(S_t,t)\,\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t^S,\qquad
d\langle W^S, W^V\rangle_t = \rho\,dt
$$

</MathBlock>

### Hedging profile

| Greek / bucket | LV | LSV |
|----------------|----|-----|
| Spot delta / gamma | From local diffusion | Similar local part + **variance exposure** |
| Vega (ATM) | Localized in $(S,t)$ | **Persists** — variance factor moves smile globally |
| Volga | Typically small in pure LV mindset | **Material** — moves in $V$ change curvature |
| Vanna | Implied by $\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}/\partial S$ | **Explicit** $\rho$ and $L$ geometry |
| Forward smile | Decays quickly | **Richer** forward skew / vol transition |

<Callout type="note">
In LSV, **choosing** $(\kappa,\theta,\xi,\rho)$ is partly a **dynamic calibration**: you match not only vanillas but also the **autocorrelation** of implied vol and the **skew stickiness** observed in the asset class.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="04" title="Forward smile and barrier P&L">

Autocalls care about $\mathbb{P}(S_{T_k} \geq B_k \mid \mathcal{A}_k)$. That probability depends on the **forward distribution** of $S_{T_k}$ given information at $T_{k-1}$ — hence on **forward implied vol** and **forward skew**.

The **model spread** in PV is often summarized as:

$$
\Delta \mathrm{NPV}_{\mathrm{LV}-\mathrm{LSV}} \approx N \cdot \text{coupon} \cdot \sum_k D(0,T_k)\, \Delta p_k
$$

For **ATM-style barriers**, LSV frequently implies **lower** autocall prices than LV (higher forward vol above spot in many calibrations) — for **down-and-risky** variants the sign can flip. Hedging must be sized to **stress** that spread, not only to the base model.

<Callout type="result" title="Hedging rule of thumb">
Overlay **forward skew hedges** (risk reversals / put spread structures in forward buckets) when LV–LSV PV differences exceed your **model reserve** threshold — otherwise barrier P&L will concentrate on observation weeks.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section number="05" title="Desk playbook — reserves and monitoring">

### Model reserve

$$
\text{Reserve} = \alpha \cdot |\Delta \mathrm{NPV}_{\mathrm{LV}-\mathrm{LSV}}|,\quad \alpha \in [0.3,0.7]
$$

### Daily monitors

| Monitor | Intent |
|---------|--------|
| LV vs LSV PV & Greeks | Bucketed vega / volga mismatch |
| Forward vol ratio | Spot vs forward consistency |
| Realized vs implied skew path | Sticky-delta vs sticky-strike behavior |
| Week-before-observation stress | Barrier proximity scenarios |

### Instrument map

- **Vanilla straddles / strangles**: ATM vega buckets
- **Risk reversals**: skew in forward slices
- **Butterflies**: volga / smile curvature
- **Short-dated OTM barriers** (when liquid): direct barrier correlation hedge

<Callout type="warning">
If **P&L explain** shows persistent **unexplained theta** into observations, first test **forward smile decay** assumptions — it is the dominant autocall hedging gap between LV and stochastic-style models.
</Callout>

</Section>
